# Mordheim Campaign Manager

A mobile-first Progressive Web App for managing Mordheim warbands and campaigns, including Border Town Burning (BTB) supplement content. Built for use at the game store — install it via "Add to Home Screen" and it behaves like a native app. Your warbands live in a Supabase Postgres database behind an account, so the same campaign follows you across phone, tablet, and desktop.

Full product spec: [`mordheim-manager-spec.md`](./mordheim-manager-spec.md).

## Status

This is under active development. Current state, milestone by milestone:

- ✅ **Scaffold** — Vite + React + TypeScript + Tailwind PWA, offline-capable, deployable to Netlify.
- ✅ **Game data** — warband definitions, equipment, skills, injuries, advance tables, wyrdstone prices, Hired Swords, Dramatis Personae, and BTB campaign objectives, sourced from the core rulebook and Border Town Burning supplement (see [Game data & verification](#game-data--verification) below).
- ✅ **Warbands** — create a warband from any of 22 warband types: the 9 core-rulebook/BTB ones; Averlanders, Beastmen Raiders, Carnival of Chaos, Dwarf Treasure Hunters, Kislevites, Orc Mob and Ostlanders from The New Mordheimer's Grade 1a lists; and Black Orcs, Amazons (Mordheim), Amazons (Lustria), Lizardmen, the Gunnery School of Nuln and the Battle Monks of Cathay from the Grade 1b/1c lists. Manage the roster (Heroes, Henchmen Groups, Hired Swords), edit stats/XP/skills/injuries/equipment, move gear to and from the treasury.
- ✅ **Post-Battle Wizard** — the 8-step guided sequence (battle info → injuries → experience → advances → dead models → income → upkeep → confirm) with a staged diff summary and single-level undo. Dice-driven steps have real roll buttons instead of requiring physical dice: D66 rolls for the Serious Injury table (with the result looked up automatically), D6 rolls for Henchmen casualties and Hired Sword fate, and full 2D6 rolls against the sourced Advance tables (auto-resolving fixed/roll-again results, presenting a choice for "either X or Y" results, and opening the skill picker for "New Skill" results).
- ✅ **Trading Post** — buy Common and Rare equipment (including warband-exclusive items) against the warband's gold, sell items back from the treasury. Rare purchases can be auto-rolled (2D6 against the item's Rare number) or resolved by hand for warband-specific modifiers. Equipment can also be bought directly from a Hero/Hired Sword or Henchmen Group's detail screen, equipping it on that model/group in one step instead of routing through the treasury.
- ✅ **Campaign Log** — start/rename a campaign and toggle Border Town Burning; a chronological, expandable log of every battle committed via the Post-Battle Wizard; a per-warband BTB objective tracker (pick an objective, free-text Campaign Points/progress notes, completed flag) shown when the campaign uses BTB.
- ✅ **Home** — a dashboard tab (first in the bottom nav, the app's `/` route) summarizing the active campaign, all warbands at a glance, and quick links into Post-Battle/Trading/Campaign. The bottom nav is icon-only on narrow phones and brings labels back at wider viewports, so the tabs don't crowd each other.
- ✅ **Pre-Battle & During-Battle** — the Battle tab now starts with setup (pick or randomly roll a scenario, note the opponent by name or by picking another warband stored in the app, pre-battle notes) before the fight, then a live tracker during it (turn counter, a timestamped event log, and a read-only quick-reference roster — stats, equipment, skills — for your own warband or the selected opponent's). Both screens persist through a reload so an accidental refresh at the table doesn't lose the game state. Finishing flows straight into the Post-Battle Wizard, which prefills the scenario/opponent from what was set up.
- ✅ **Skills system** — the 5 core skill lists (Combat, Shooting, Academic, Strength, Speed) plus each warband's unique list are wired into a shared skill picker used by both the model detail screens and the Post-Battle Wizard's Advances step. It hard-blocks skills a Hero already knows or definitely isn't eligible for (leader-only, excluded warband types) and surfaces prerequisites it can't verify (e.g. "Spellcasters only") as a warning instead of guessing.
- ✅ **Rules Reference** — a searchable, offline rules browser covering the full core rulebook: Characteristics, the Turn sequence, Movement, Shooting, Close Combat, Wounds & Injuries, Leadership & Psychology, Weapons & Armour, Miscellaneous Equipment, Magic/casting (plus the wizard-to-lore allocation table), the Post-Battle Sequence, Serious Injuries, Experience (including the Underdog XP bonus table), Trading & Hired Swords, and — generated live from the app's own data rather than duplicated — Skills, Scenarios, Warband-Specific Rules, and Border Town Burning objectives/Dramatis Personae. Every entry is grouped and ordered to match the real rulebook's chapter structure and cites the exact page it came from. Entries cross-link to related rules, and confirmed gaps (the core rulebook's own Dramatis Personae, the more elaborate Optional Rules variants, full scenario terrain/deployment text) are flagged explicitly instead of guessed. The per-lore spell lists that were once a gap here are now filled — all 30 lists live under the Magic category (see below).
- ✅ **Rules woven into their home tabs** — Warbands, Trading, and Campaign each have a "Rules" toggle alongside their normal view, surfacing just the rules relevant to that tab (warband creation & each faction's special rules; trading & Hired Swords; the post-battle/campaign rules, Serious Injuries, Experience, and Border Town Burning) without leaving the screen. The full Rules Reference (with search) is still always available from its own tab too.
- ✅ **PWA polish** — the iOS-specific "Add to Home Screen" meta tags (standalone status bar, home screen title) alongside the existing Android/manifest support, plus a proper `<meta name="description">`. Precaching an app shell made sense when warbands lived in `localStorage`, but after the move to Supabase it bought nothing and cost real confusion: an old bundle kept being served out of `workbox-precache-v2` after a deploy had landed, making a correct release look broken until the user cleared site data. That precache manifest was removed, and for a while the worker was disabled outright. **Superseded** — the worker is back, built the opposite way round; see the caching entry below for what it does now.
- ✅ **Responsive tablet/desktop layout** — below `md` (mobile) the app keeps its bottom tab bar and full-width screens. At `md` and up a left sidebar takes over: an icon-only rail on tablet that expands to icons + labels on desktop, with the app wordmark at the top. The screen content sits in a centered, readable max-width column beside it instead of stretching edge-to-edge. Both navs are generated from one shared list of tabs/icons so they never drift apart.
- ✅ **Supabase backend, accounts & multi-device sync** — the app moved off browser `localStorage` onto Supabase Postgres as the single source of truth, so a campaign is no longer trapped on one device. Email/password accounts gate everything that touches *your* data; every screen reads and writes through TanStack Query hooks (`src/hooks`) over a thin API layer (`src/api`). Nine tables (`profiles`, `campaigns`, `campaign_members`, `warbands`, `battles`, `objectives`, `campaign_events`, plus `issue_reports` and `admins`) all have row-level security, so you can only ever see your own data or a campaign you've been invited to. Warband saves use optimistic concurrency on `updated_at` — if the same warband was changed elsewhere, the save is rejected rather than silently clobbering the other edit. The post-battle undo snapshot moved server-side too, becoming per-warband instead of one global slot. BTB objectives live in their own owner-only table rather than inside the warband blob, since warbands are shareable and objectives are secret by the rules.
- ✅ **Public rules, private data** — the reference half of the app needs no account: the home page, the full Rules Reference and every rule entry (skills included), and the changelog are all open, so the rules are usable at the table by anyone. An account is only required for the parts that read or write your own rows — warbands (list, create, roster, model/henchmen detail), the pre/during/post-battle flow, the Trading Post, the campaign log, and data export/import. Signed out, the home page becomes a short landing view pointing at the rules and the sign-in/register links, and Settings hides export/import behind a prompt rather than offering buttons that can't work.
- ✅ **Static About + changelog page** — `public/about.html` is a plain static page (a sibling of the marketing landing page, linked from its footer), holding a short "what this is" and the full changelog, newest first. It's *generated* at build time by `scripts/build-about.mjs` from the same `src/data/changelog.json` the in-app changelog reads — so there's one source of truth and no runtime JS or data fetch on the page. Its styling is lifted verbatim from `landing.html`'s token block, so the two front doors can't drift, and the theme toggle + no-flash pre-paint match the rest of the site.
- ✅ **Public warband gallery** — the `public` visibility flag finally has a consumer. The Warbands tab gains a **Public** view listing every shared warband with its owner, type and rating, searchable by warband name, player name, or the *display* name of the type (searching "possessed" finds a `cult-of-the-possessed` warband, which matching the stored slug alone would miss), plus a type filter that only offers types actually present in the results. Rows link to the same read-only roster the campaign standings use, now at the neutral `/rosters/:id` since a shared warband isn't necessarily a campaign one. Fixed alongside it: `warbandType` stores the definition slug, but six screens rendered it raw, so the app showed "cult-of-the-possessed" wherever it meant "Cult of the Possessed". A single `getWarbandTypeName` helper resolves it, falling back to the raw value so an unrecognised or legacy type still shows something.
- ✅ **Two themes, and the app's own icon** — Settings → Appearance offers **Grimdark** (the default: near-black stone, bone text, ember accent) and **Rulebook** (aged paper, black serif ink, dark sepia accent — the printed book, and far easier to read under bright light). The palette moved behind CSS variables so a theme is a set of variable values rather than a rewrite of the ~1200 utility classes that reference it; the surface *roles* are unchanged, so `ink-950` is still "the page" and `ink-900` still "a card" — they're simply both light in Rulebook, with `ink-800/700` becoming darker rules on paper instead of lighter edges in the dark. Applied by an inline script before first paint so a Rulebook user never sees a flash of the dark theme, and stored per device: reading at a bright table in a game store is a different problem from reading at home, and it shouldn't follow the account to another screen. Contrast was measured rather than eyeballed — every text/background pair clears WCAG AA in both themes, most at AAA. Also fixes `bone-400`, used in 29 places but never defined, so those hints silently inherited their parent's colour instead of going dimmer. The home-screen icon is generated from a single source into the sizes Android and iOS need, with the maskable variant re-cropped to sit inside the 80% safe zone (the artwork had 6% top margin and would have lost the sword hilt to a circular mask).
- ✅ **Explicit saving** — typed fields used to write on every keystroke, so correcting a characteristic fired one save per digit and there was no moment at which the user decided the edit was finished; a mis-tap had no undo, and a dropped connection surfaced as an alert mid-typing. The roster, hero/Hired Sword, henchmen and campaign screens now hold a local draft with a save bar that appears only when something is pending. Discrete actions — buying, recruiting, selling, deleting, recording an advance or injury — deliberately still write immediately, since they *are* the user's decision and putting a second Save behind a Buy button reads as the purchase not having happened. Those actions apply on top of the current draft rather than beside it, so a screen never holds two competing versions of the warband and pending field edits are carried along instead of being lost. Leaving the tab with unsaved changes prompts.
- ✅ **Caching that can't strand a deploy** — query results are held for 30s with a 5-minute retention, so moving around your own roster stops refetching on every tap (window focus and mutations still invalidate, so a campaign-mate's change lands promptly). The service worker is back, but built the opposite way round from the one that broke two releases: **no precache manifest at all**, the HTML shell on NetworkFirst so a new deploy wins the moment there's a connection, hashed `/assets/` on CacheFirst because their contents can never change, and Supabase calls explicitly uncached — your warbands must never come from a stale copy. Navigation is cached under a single key, since Netlify rewrites every path to the same HTML; without that, opening `/rules` offline would miss unless that exact URL had been visited online. The one thing that quietly defeated all of this was the leftover cleanup helper: it deleted every cache whose name began `workbox-precache`, and the *current* worker's precache is named `workbox-precache-v2-<origin>` — the same prefix — so it wiped the live precache on every page load. It's gone; workbox already reconciles its own precache against the new manifest on install, which is what removes a stale entry. Verified by stopping the server and loading a deep link that had never been opened: the full Rules Reference rendered with all 20 chapters.
- ✅ **Battle flow** — the pre-battle opponent picker offers every warband entered in your campaign alongside your own, grouped and labelled with the owning player since two people can field the same warband type; picking one resolves through the shared read-only path, because another player's warband isn't in the `owner_id = me` query the screen was using. Casualties are recorded as they happen: Heroes and Hired Swords toggle out of action, henchmen groups get a counter (a group is N models behind one card, so *how many* went down is the only answerable question), and the tally seeds the post-battle Injuries step instead of being reconstructed from memory afterwards. Only out-of-action carries over — who *died* is a post-battle roll — and it's re-derived against the current warband, so ids that no longer exist are dropped and group counts clamped. Stepping the turn counter back is confirmed, since it's the one number nobody can reconstruct and the button sits a thumb's width from "+".
- ✅ **Laid out like the book** — equipment is grouped under the price chart's own headings in the price chart's own order (hand-to-hand, missile, armour, miscellaneous) in the shop, the treasury and everywhere else gear is listed, so looking something up matches the muscle memory of the printed page; an unrecognised category falls into Miscellaneous rather than vanishing, so a data typo surfaces as a misfiled item. Henchmen are listed out individually beneath their group — the rules treat a group as one entity and the data holds a count, but "4x Swordsman" doesn't read like four bodies on the table — while everything editable stays on the group, and their Experience is labelled "each" because it isn't a group total. The Rules Reference browses as a collapsible chapter list with per-chapter counts; search deliberately stays flat, since collapsing would hide the matches. The disclosure arrow is a real SVG instead of a `▶` text glyph that reflowed differently in every substituted system font.
- ✅ **Exploration tables** — the full Exploration chart (all 30 double-through-six-of-a-kind results, with their sub-tables and item checklists), the wyrdstone shards table and the magical artefacts table, transcribed from the owner's own rulebook and browsable in the Rules Reference.
- ✅ **Exploration in the post-battle wizard** — the Income step now rolls the chart instead of asking for a wyrdstone total from memory. The dice pool is derived from the draft (one per Hero who survived without going out of action, plus one for a win) and can be adjusted for skills or equipment that grant more; each die is a picker as well as a roll button, since the rules expect players to witness each other's dice and an Elf Ranger's +1/-1 or a Mordheim Map re-roll has to land somewhere. Past six dice the extras become tappable Counts/Dropped chips, because the rulebook keeps only six however many you rolled. Multiples are matched with the book's own tie-breaks (most numerous wins, then highest face), and the result is resolved *for your warband* — Skaven sell the Straggler for 2D6 gc where everyone else interrogates him, Witch Hunters seal the Graveyard for Experience where everyone else loots it for D6x10. What the app applies on its own is deliberately limited to gold and shards, declared as an `autoYield` dice expression in the data rather than parsed out of the prose; everything else (a Zombie, a wardog, a free Hired Sword, a blessed weapon) is written into the battle notes for the player, and effects that outlast the battle are appended to the warband's notes as well.
- ✅ **Six more warbands, and a regression they exposed** — Black Orcs, both Amazon lists, Lizardmen, the Gunnery School of Nuln and the Battle Monks of Cathay, each with hero and henchmen entries, equipment lists, unit special rules, their own skill table and their unique gear. Adding them was also the first time every warband's data was validated end to end, which turned up a fault I had introduced myself: seven older warbands list `mace` and `hammer` as separate ids while the price chart carries one combined `clubMaceHammer` entry, and Maneaters reference a `cleaver` that never existed. Harmless while equipment lists were only descriptive — but the previous change made the shop *filter* by them, so Reiklanders, Middenheimers, Marienburgers, Witch Hunters, Sisters of Sigmar, Undead and the Cult silently lost the ability to buy a club at all. 27 dead ids repaired, and the validation is worth keeping as a check whenever warband data changes. Two Amazon warbands also meant the equipment locks added earlier, which pointed at a single `amazons` id, had to name both.
- ✅ **Weapon slot limits** — two close combat weapons plus the free dagger, and two *different* missile weapons, per the rulebook's equipment section. Both details in that sentence matter: the free dagger is identified by costing nothing, so a bought one still counts against the limit, and counting distinct missile *names* is what makes "a brace of pistols counts as a single missile weapon" fall out naturally — a second pistol is the same weapon, not a third slot. Armour and miscellaneous gear stay uncapped. Enforced on both routes onto a model (buying and assigning from the treasury), and shown as `1/2 close combat, 0/2 missile` above the equipment list so a full slot is visible before shopping rather than at the point of refusal. The treasury is deliberately exempt: the rules cap what a warrior *carries*, not what the warband owns. Warbands already over the limit keep what they have — the check guards additions only, since silently discarding bought gear isn't the app's call.
- ✅ **Several campaigns per player** — the schema always allowed it (`campaign_members` is keyed on campaign *and* user for exactly this reason) and joining several already worked, but the create form lived in the no-campaign branch of the campaign screen, so a leader was stuck with the first one they made. A new **My Campaigns** screen at `/campaigns` is now the Campaign tab's destination, listing every campaign with your role, its player and battle counts, how many of your warbands are entered, and — for leaders only, since nobody else can hand it out — the join code. Both the join field and the start form sit on that screen rather than linking away to the campaign detail. Two navigation faults surfaced while wiring it, of the same kind: `NavLink` matches on a tab's own path, so the Campaign tab went dark the moment you opened a campaign (`/campaign` isn't a prefix of `/campaigns`), and the Warbands tab claimed the entire battle flow, which lives under `/warbands/:id/…` because it belongs to a warband. Nav items can now both claim and *exclude* paths, with exclusion winning over the built-in prefix match.
- ✅ **Campaign collaboration set** — five features built on the shared-campaign RLS, all reusing the `campaign_events` policy shape rather than inventing new patterns. **Leader announcements** (migration 0018): a single pinned banner per campaign, stored as two columns on `campaigns` so it needs no new table — reading rides the members-or-public select policy, writing the leader-only update policy. **Narrative log** (0017): story entries between games, distinct from the battle tally, each optionally linked to a recent battle, on the campaign Log tab. **Event RSVPs** (0019): Going/Maybe/Can't per game night with a self-only write, a per-status roster, and a `going · maybe` tally on the next-event banner. **Territory control** (0020): a fourth campaign tab tracking who holds what — a shared, members-writable board (territory changes hands at the table, so no leader-approval step), with `ON DELETE SET NULL` so a departing warband's territory returns to unclaimed, and a type-to-confirm removal. **Scenario generator**: a weighted "Suggest a scenario" button in the pre-battle flow, gated so a story scenario can't be suggested before the campaign has earned it, only ever filling the field the manual picker fills.
- ✅ **Magic expanded to every caster** — `spells.json` grew from 10 lists to **30 (182 spells)**: the original curated ten plus twenty converted from the Mordheimer Sourcedata, with the scraped `<Tooltip>`/`<br/>`/`<table>` markup and `�` mojibake scrubbed at conversion. **26 caster hero slots** across the imported warbands are now wired to their lists — the Arabian Mystic, Chaos-Dwarf Sorcerers, Dark Elf Sorceress, Bretonnian Damsel, Restless Dead Liche/Necromancer, and the Marauder Seer (who draws on all four Mark-of-Chaos ritual lists, since the data can't capture which Mark the warband took). All 30 lists are also browsable and searchable in the Rules Reference under a new **Magic** category, which previously had nothing behind its filter — searching "Doombolt" now lands on the Dark Elf spell with its casting difficulty. This closes the "per-lore spell lists" gap the Rules Reference had flagged.
- ✅ **Dice roller in reach from anywhere** — a floating dice button stacks directly under the how-to `?` in the top corner and opens the standalone `DiceRoller` in a dismissible overlay (backdrop / X / Escape), so a house-rule roll never means leaving the screen you're on. It reuses the same component the `/dice` route and the during-battle tracker embed, and is public like the route it mirrors.
- ✅ **Equipment rules, restrictions and unit special rules** — three separate faults, found by auditing rather than guessing. Warband-exclusive gear could *never* display its rules: the name→rule index was built only from the universal catalogue, so the Ogre club, hand-held mortar and harpoon crossbow rendered as plain rows while their full text sat unused in the data. Indexing exclusive gear by id and display name surfaced **42 items** at a stroke; a `TODO:` placeholder deliberately still resolves to nothing, so a data gap can't pose as rules. The catalogue then grew **60 → 142** with the whole Miscellaneous Equipment section, of which 36 items are locked to warbands the app doesn't carry yet — present and searchable, correct the day that warband lands, and filtered out of everyone else's shop by `warbandIds`. Purchasing now follows the book: a Henchmen group buys one item *per model* (it charged once regardless of size, so five swordsmen cost one sword), Miscellaneous gear is Heroes-only bar the exceptions the rules name, and each warrior sees only his own entry's list — widened by Weapons Training for hand-to-hand and Weapons Expert for missiles, verified to open the right category and only that one. The treasury is deliberately exempt, because the rules restrict *use*, not purchase. Finally, unit special rules moved out of the free-text `notes` blob into a structured, expandable list — 111 rules across 61 units. Mechanical splitting needed three corrections to be trustworthy: lower-case data remarks weren't recognised as boundaries and bled into the preceding rule; rules written without a colon ("Large (causes fear...)") were being swallowed whole, so one Maneaters rule claimed four unrelated things; and a bare rule is a single sentence, so anything after it belongs elsewhere. What's left unsplit stays visible under a "to do" badge rather than being dropped or dressed up as finished.
- ✅ **§5.4 audited on the real screens** — the design sandbox can't see the app chrome or the detail screens, and four genuine violations were only visible once signed in and measuring the live DOM. `BackHeader` set its blackletter title at 20px, under the §5.2 floor of 24 that exists because blackletter stops being readable below it. Tab-bar labels sat at 11px, under the 12px floor. Inline actions — "Move to treasury", the weapon-rule expanders, checkboxes, the changelog link — ranged from 16 to 24px tall. Worst was the characteristic field at **15px**: the control you most need to hit one-handed at a table was the smallest thing in the app. All are now at or above 44px, and nothing anywhere renders below 12px. Deliberately left alone: a 36–40px convention on tab buttons, Buy buttons and rules filters. §5.4 asks for 48px+, so these fall short, but raising them lengthens dense screens (the trading post has 26) — a density trade-off worth making on purpose rather than by sweep.
- ✅ **The "Rulebook" design language (spec §5)** — aged parchment, heavy woodcut ink, blackletter display type and blood red, sitting alongside the existing Grimdark theme rather than replacing it. The §5.1 tokens are defined per theme so one component works in both: `parchment` means "the page" and `ink` means "text on it", which under Grimdark resolves to bone-on-black. Contrast was computed rather than eyeballed — ink on parchment 12.80:1, white on blood 10.35:1, both clearing WCAG AA with room to spare. Measuring *both* themes turned up a trap the spec's own wording hides: §5 requires white on blood, but Grimdark's accent is a light ember orange where white manages only 3.76:1, so a shared component cannot hardcode white. A paired `on-accent` token resolves to whatever is legible on the accent in the active theme. The §5.2 type system (Pirata One, IM Fell English, Alegreya, Alegreya Sans) is applied once as a base layer rather than opted into by each of fifty-odd files, and is global to both themes — fonts aren't colours, and two parallel type systems would double the work for nothing. The signature element is the profile block: a real `<table>`, not a grid of divs, so a screen reader announces "WS 4" instead of a bare "4"; tabular *and* lining figures, because Alegreya's default oldstyle numerals drop 3/4/5/9 below the baseline and render 0 at x-height, which makes a statline's zeros read as the letter o; and cell padding, never font size, is what shrinks to fit nine columns onto a 375px phone. It's used everywhere a statline appears, editable on the detail screens, which also retired stat labels set at 9 and 10px — under the §5.4 floor.
- ✅ **Shared UI kit (design-system unification)** — the tokens and fonts were always consistent, but the *components* weren't: the same button was hand-written ~11 ways (min-h 40/44/48/52, three different `disabled:opacity` values), the card surface 20-odd ways, and errors sometimes used a raw `text-red-400` that neither theme's palette owns. `src/components/ui/` settles each into one primitive — `Button` (primary/secondary/danger/ghost, md/dense), `Card`, `SectionHeading`/`Eyebrow`, `Field`/`TextField`/`Textarea`/`Select` — so a screen can't drift from a shape it no longer spells out. The primary button's label goes through the `on-accent` token rather than a hardcoded colour, so it's correctly near-black on Grimdark's ember and white on Rulebook's blood. Validated in both themes in the `/design` sandbox, which carries a live gallery of the kit. **Every product screen is now migrated** — auth, account, campaign (including CampaignScreen's own large body), warband/roster/detail, battle, trading, rules, and the post-battle wizard. The admin screens, which had been written in the full parchment/ink idiom, were reconciled to the app's dark tokens (a border-and-fill swap, since the role tokens already resolved correctly in both themes). The static landing page was already mirroring the §5.1 token values with its own theme toggle, so it shares the design without importing the bundle.
- ✅ **Custom warbands (clone & rename, spec §21.2 narrow scope)** — a user can clone any built-in warband as a custom type, rename it and its units, and reassign its limits (starting gold, min/max size, per-unit recruit caps), then build warbands from it like any other. Deliberately *not* a from-scratch builder: §3.3 forbids inventing stat lines or prices, so a custom type is a whole `WarbandDefinition` copied from a bundled one with only names and numbers reassigned — the definition is stored (denormalised) in `custom_warband_types` as jsonb, owner-scoped by RLS (migration 0021), so a later correction to the base can't retroactively change a warband already built on the old numbers. The hard part was integration: a runtime `customById` map in `warbandRegistry` that `getWarbandDefinition` falls back to (populated once the signed-in user's types load), so the factory, roster, health check and rules resolver all read a custom type exactly like a bundled one, and the New Warband picker lists them under "Your custom types". Types sync across the owner's devices; sharing one with campaign-mates (so they can read a shared roster built on it) is the documented next step, which the owner-only policy leaves for later.
- ✅ **Campaign activity feed** — the Log tab opens with *Recent activity*, one reverse-chronological timeline merging battles, narrative-log entries, scheduled game nights, the pinned announcement and territory claims. Built entirely from data the campaign screen already loads, merged client-side rather than through a new feed table, since every source carries its own timestamp; RSVPs are the one activity left out, having no campaign-wide query yet.
- ✅ **Shareable warband cards** — a "Share card" button on the roster renders the warband to a PNG (name, type, rating, sectioned roster, treasury, branding, best-effort photo) drawn straight onto a canvas, the same dependency-free approach as the photo pipeline and for the same reason (full control over the self-hosted fonts). The Web Share sheet takes it into Discord on a phone; a download everywhere else.
- ✅ **Head-to-head match logs** — each opponent on the Rivalries card expands to the full head-to-head: every game with its date, scenario and result, plus the total wyrdstone found across the rivalry, extending the running W/L/D tally that was already there.
- ✅ **Table tools — injury rolls, warband check, loadouts** — the during-battle screen gains an Injury rolls panel (the D6 wound result and the D66 serious-injury table, each logged into the event log). Every roster carries a *Warband check* — `checkWarband` surfaces, in one place, what was previously computed only at the moment of each action: over the size limit, too many of a unit, an advance earned but unspent (accounting for advances baked into starting profiles), and upkeep due next battle. Roster cards now also list each fighter's weapons, skills and special rules by name, and weapon cost/rarity is suppressed where it's noise (the during-battle reference) via a `hidePricing` prop on the shared weapon view.
- ✅ **Advances follow the Experience track** — the wizard offered an advance to every model that gained any XP at all, which is why it handed out far more than a campaign should; `advancesDue()` now counts the Experience boxes actually crossed between the pre- and post-battle totals, so gaining a point from 2 to 3 correctly yields nothing while 1 to 4 yields two. Racial maximums are enforced rather than annotated — the previous ⚠ was advisory and still applied the increase — and staged increases count toward the cap, so two advances in one battle can't both be spent on a characteristic with one point of headroom. A rolled characteristic that's already maxed reports that a re-roll is due instead of being silently wasted, matching the rule quoted in `advances.json`. Every staged advance is removable, since rolling was otherwise irreversible and a mis-tap meant restarting the wizard. Dead heroes, wiped-out henchmen groups and Hired Swords killed in battle no longer collect XP or advances. "That Lad's Got Talent" is recordable at last — its handler was an empty function, so the result could not even be clicked — and resolves on commit by moving one member out of the group into the Heroes with his characteristics and Experience intact; which skill lists he may use varies by warband and isn't in the data, so that's left to the player rather than guessed.
- ✅ **Recruiting obeys the rules** — heroes, henchmen and Hired Swords all cost gold, and the warband's maximum size is enforced alongside each unit's own limit: a henchmen group could previously be sized straight past it, so 30 models fitted into a warband capped at 15 without a word. Full units grey out rather than vanishing, since a silently missing option reads as a bug. Hired Swords are recruitable at last — fee, upkeep, statline, eligibility and special rules shown before you commit — and they correctly sit *outside* the size cap, which is why `countTowardWarbandSize` is deliberately separate from `countModels` in `rating.ts`: rating counts them, roster capacity doesn't. Every warrior starts with the rulebook's free dagger, and each recruit now carries its unit's own rules text (Leader, Large, Ranger, …) which the factory previously discarded, leaving abilities a model has from the moment you hire him nowhere on his card. Warband lists are ordered A–Z and labelled with source and grade, derived from the `source` citation each data file already carries rather than a second hand-maintained field that could drift. Also fixes a rating bug: a henchmen group's Experience is per model — they advance together and share one value — but was counted once for the whole group, understating five 4-XP veterans as 29 instead of 45.
- ✅ **Public warbands need no account** — a roster marked public is now readable signed out, which is the point of publishing one; a link you can't open without registering isn't shared. Enforced in the database rather than the UI: migration 0004 grants `anon` select on warbands whose `visibility = 'public'` and on `profiles` (display names only — the table holds no contact details), leaving the owner and campaign-member branches on the authenticated policy. Verified signed out: public warbands and names readable, private warbands/campaigns/battles/objectives all return zero rows.
- ✅ **No campaign appears by itself** — committing a battle used to silently create a campaign named "My Campaign", because `battles` had nowhere to file a row without one, so every player ended up leading a campaign they never started. Migration 0005 lets a battle exist with no campaign (personal, readable only by its reporter), which also delivers one-off games outside a campaign. The join-code field now sits permanently on the Campaign screen instead of under the Players tab, where nobody found it once they already had a campaign — which read as "you can only be in one".
- ✅ **Reliable edits and typable number fields** — two fixes to things that made ordinary use feel broken. Every warband write carries the `updated_at` it last saw so a second device can't silently clobber your changes, but two saves from the *same* tab were indistinguishable from that: buying an item and buying another before the first response landed meant the second still held the pre-purchase timestamp, so it was refused and the user was told their warband "was changed elsewhere" when nothing of the sort had happened — and the purchase was lost. Saves are now queued per warband and each writes its fresh timestamp straight into the cache, so a run of edits lands in order while the concurrency check still catches a genuinely concurrent editor. Separately, every number field was spelled `value={n} onChange={set(clamp(Number(e.target.value)))}`, which forces each keystroke back through a number and so can never be empty: clearing the field to type a new value snapped it to the minimum under the cursor, and `Number('')` being 0 (with `Math.max(1, NaN)` being NaN) meant a half-typed entry could reach the warband as 0 or NaN. A single `NumberInput` now holds the in-progress text and interprets it only when it parses cleanly and sits in range, rounding and clamping on blur — used by the henchmen group size, the buy and sell price overrides, characteristics, wyrdstone, and the post-battle casualty counts.
- ✅ **Shared campaigns** — a campaign is no longer a private notebook. Creating one issues a short, WhatsApp-shareable join code (`MRDH-7F2K`, drawn from an alphabet with the confusable characters removed — no `0`/`O`, `1`/`I`/`L`, `5`/`S`, `8`/`B` — since these get read aloud and typed on phones); the leader can regenerate it to close off further joins. Codes are matched case- and punctuation-insensitively, so `mrdh7f2k` works. Players enter a code from the Campaign tab (on the create-or-join screen if they have no campaign, or under Players if they already do) and are switched into the campaign they joined. The code can be pushed straight into the group chat: WhatsApp gets a real share link (`wa.me/?text=`), while Discord — which has no share-intent URL, so nothing can open it with a message prefilled — gets a button that copies a Discord-formatted message instead of pretending otherwise. Both carry the app's URL alongside the code, since a bare code is useless to someone who hasn't been told where to enter it. A warband is entered into a campaign from its own roster screen, and then appears in **Standings**. That table is built from the member list outwards rather than from the linked warbands: listing warbands and reading the player off each one is the obvious approach but drops anyone who hasn't entered one — most often the leader, who tends to set the campaign up before building a roster, and so appeared nowhere in their own campaign. Every player is now listed with their name, warband, rating and W/L/D read off the shared battle log, with a Leader badge and an explicit "no warband entered" where that applies, and any member can open a read-only view of anyone's roster (including their own, which is labelled as the view everyone else gets rather than as someone else's warband). Campaign name/BTB/notes are editable by the leader only, matching what RLS already enforced. BTB objectives stay owner-only regardless of who can see the warband. Two database-level guarantees back this up rather than trusting the client: a warband can only be linked to a campaign its owner actually belongs to (a `WITH CHECK` the original schema was missing, without which a client could have pushed its warband into any campaign it knew the id of), and leaving or being removed from a campaign withdraws that player's warbands from it via trigger, since the leader has no write access to someone else's rows.
- ✅ **Sign in/out from the nav, and password recovery** — the navigation carries a single control that reads "Sign in" or "Sign out" depending on session state, pinned to the bottom of the sidebar rail on tablet/desktop. On the mobile tab bar only **Sign in** appears: signing out lives on Settings, which already carries it under Account next to the address you're signed in as, so the bar was spending one of eight cells on the destructive action — a thumb's width from Settings itself — to duplicate something one tap away. Removing it gives the seven remaining tabs ~54px each instead of ~47px on a 375px phone. Signing *in* stays, since it's the entry point for someone with no session, and the cell is free exactly when they need it. It stays blank while the session is still being restored, so a signed-in user reloading the page never sees a "Sign in" flash. Signing in remembers the page you came from and returns you there. A forgotten-password flow now runs end to end: a link on the sign-in screen leads to a request form, Supabase emails a recovery link back to `/reset-password`, and that screen sets the new password against the temporary recovery session (with expired/reused links reported plainly). The request form deliberately shows the same "if an account exists" message whether or not the address is registered, so it can't be used to discover who has an account. The standalone Skills tab was retired — all 133 skill entries still live in the Rules Reference under their own chapter, which is where people were looking for them anyway.
- ✅ **Magic, prayers and rituals** — ten lists and 60 entries (`spells.json`), transcribed from The Mordheimer: Prayers of Sigmar and of Taal, Chaos, Nurgle and Amazon rituals, Lesser Magic, Lizardmen magic, Magic of the Horned Rat, Necromancy and Waaagh! magic. Wired to twelve caster hero slots and the Warlock hired sword. A caster gets a block between skills and equipment, headed by what the player's own book calls the thing — a Sigmarite Matriarch prays, she does not cast spells — with the casting difficulty on the collapsed row because that's the figure needed at the table. The model rolls in-app *or* the player picks, side by side, exactly as injuries and advances already work; duplicates re-roll automatically and say that they did, per the rulebook's own instruction. Choosing used to be a native `<select>`, which on a phone hands off to the OS picker and reduces each entry to one truncated line — so the only way to learn what a spell did was to take it and read it afterwards. It's now a disclosure list where each entry opens its own rules text, sub-table and errata, with learning as a separate button so reading can't accidentally commit. Two entries succeed automatically rather than being cast against a number, and the source's own disputed passages are carried as errata so a contested rule reads as contested. The lists are resolved from the game data rather than from a copy stored on the model — which is what was hiding magic on every caster recruited before the feature existed.
- ✅ **One racial maximums table** — the ceiling a warrior may advance to now lives once (`racialMaximums.json`, 29 profiles) with each unit pointing at one, instead of 125 units each restating the same nine numbers. That was mostly de-duplication, but it closed a real hole: **31 units had no maximums at all**, and an absent maximum resolved to a line of zeroes, which reads as "every characteristic is already at its cap" and blocked every advance those models could ever take. Every unit that *did* carry hand-entered numbers already agreed with its profile, so nothing else changed behaviour. A unit that cannot gain Experience deliberately gets no profile — it never advances, so a ceiling is meaningless and inventing one implies otherwise — which the resolver reports as null rather than as zeroes. Three Carnival of Chaos entries keep their own numbers, having no published race. Also fixes "That Lad's Got Talent", which handed the promoted Hero *his own current stats* as his maximums and so froze him permanently at the numbers he was promoted with.
- ✅ **Special rules shared instead of retranscribed** — Leader appeared in all 22 warbands in eleven different wordings, Fear in six warbands in three, Wizard in nine in seven. They are the same rulebook rules, so a correction had to be made in every copy to take effect and drift between copies was invisible. The twelve that genuinely recur now live in `specialRules.json` and units refer to them by id, replacing 67 inline copies. Sharing them doesn't flatten real differences: a reference can override a placeholder — the Gunnery School's and Reikland's Leader really do reach 12" where everyone else's reaches 6" — and can carry a warband's own rider, which kept eight of them, including the Skink Priest's replacement clause, the Goblins' Animosity exception and the Skaven Hero exemption from Stupidity. One was left inline on purpose: the Beastmen Minotaur's "Animal" means the opposite of everyone else's — it *grants* Experience — so collapsing it into the shared "never gains Experience" would have been wrong. The other 114 genuinely unique rules are untouched, and references resolve in the registry so no screen knows a rule can be stored two ways.
- ✅ **Report an issue, and an admin view for what comes in** — a "Report an issue" button at the foot of every screen opens a textbox in place, and files the report to an `issue_reports` table rather than opening a mail client. Filing rather than emailing is the point: the row carries the path you were on, the build, the user agent and a small context blob (which warband type, which unit), so a report reads "the Necromancer has no spells" *with* `{warbandType: undead, unitType: Necromancer}` attached instead of arriving as prose you have to interview someone about. It works signed out — the rules are public, so a stranger finding a wrong weapon price is exactly who you want to hear from — and insert is anonymous-friendly while **reading** is admin-only, so nobody can enumerate other people's reports. An `/admin` screen carries the inbox (filter by open/triaged/closed, expand for the captured context, mark triaged or closed) and aggregate statistics from a Postgres `admin_stats()` function: players, warbands, public warbands, campaigns, battles, open issues, a 30-day signup series, and the warband-type distribution — which is the one number actually worth having, since it tells you which lists people play. Aggregates only, deliberately: no row access to anyone's warband and nothing touching objectives, because owner-only objectives are the reason that table is separate at all. Admin is a row in an `admins` table checked by a `SECURITY DEFINER` function, so the gate is in the database and an unlinked route is not what protects it.
- ✅ **Settings became Account** — the tab was called Settings but holds your account, your theme and your data. It went via "Profile" before landing on "Account", which is what it actually is; every old path still resolves (`/settings`, `/profile`, and both `…/changelog` variants redirect), so bookmarks and links shared in the group chat keep working. Admins get a link to `/admin` from there, shown only when the database says so — the screen was previously reachable only by typing the URL. The sign-out button also left the mobile tab bar: it was spending one of eight cells on a phone, on the destructive action, a thumb's width from the tab itself, to duplicate something already one tap away. Signing *in* stays in the bar, since that's the entry point for someone with no session and the cell is free exactly when they need it.
- ✅ **Campaign awards & rivalries** — two flourishes on the campaign Standings tab, both pure-derived from the shared battle log with no new table. **Awards** hand out four titles as the campaign runs — Wyrdstone Baron (most shards), On a Roll (longest win streak), Old Campaigner (most battles), Top Dogs (highest rating) — each a snapshot of the current standings rather than a locked-in achievement, and each shown only once it has a real holder so a fresh campaign crowns nobody. "Bloodiest" was left out on purpose: it needs a structured casualty count, and `casualtiesSummary` is free text, so counting models out of a prose sentence would be a guess dressed as a statistic. **Rivalries** tally your warband's head-to-head W/L/D against every opponent it has faced. Grouped by opponent *name*, not warband id — the pasted spec assumed a match by id, but `BattleRecord.opponents` stores the names picked in the pre-battle flow, never the opponent's warband id, so the derived view is what the data supports; the persisted "nemesis" star it also proposed waits for the battle record to start capturing opponent ids.
- ✅ **Roster & model depth** — three touches that make individual warriors feel lived-in, none touching rating or rules. **Nicknames** on heroes and hired swords, shown in parentheses after the name via one shared helper so the roster row, print sheet and shared roster can't drift. **Epitaphs**: a dead hero's last words, captured in the post-battle wizard — and folded into the battle's casualty summary rather than a roster field, because this app *removes* a dead hero from the roster on commit, so the spec's plan to store it on the killing injury had nothing to attach to; the battle log is where a fallen hero's memory belongs anyway. A per-model **equipment history** under the gear block, written as a side effect of the actions that already move gear (buying onto a model, assigning from or moving to the treasury), filling forward from empty rather than inventing a past. And a **rating-over-time chart** on the warband detail screen — a hand-drawn SVG line (no chart library, matching the admin sparkline), fed by a trigger-written `warband_rating_history` table (migration 0016) that records a point whenever rating actually changes, shown only once there are two points to draw a line between. The dice roller is also now embedded, collapsible, on the during-battle screen — you roll constantly during a game, and a link would pull you off the live tracker.
- ✅ **Three table-side utilities** — a standalone **dice roller** (`/dice`, reached from the Rules screen), a **warband comparison** tool (`/compare`, two warbands' profiles side by side), and a **"what can I afford"** filter on the trading post. All three are the cheapest tier of the feature backlog: no schema, no new tables, nothing server-side. The dice roller is a UI wrapper around the `lib/dice.ts` the app already rolls injuries and advances with — D3/D6/D66/2D6/D100 with a count and a modifier, a big result and a session history — and it's public, since a roller behind a login is useless at a table where not everyone has an account. Compare resolves each warband through the same RLS-gated path the read-only shared roster uses, so it works for your own, a campaign-mate's and any public one, and refuses a private warband exactly as opening it would. The afford filter narrows the catalogue to what the warband's gold can cover, using a rare item's *lowest* possible price since the real one isn't known until it's rolled, and never hides an item whose price can't be computed — hiding something you might afford is worse than showing one you can't.
- ✅ **A smaller first load** — the entry bundle went from one 1,112 kB chunk to 666 kB (180 kB gzipped). Routes off the first-paint path are lazy, while Home, the warband list and the auth screens stay eager, since splitting those trades a smaller download for a spinner on the one path everybody takes. The larger win was subtler: `rulesIndex` pulls in rules.json, the whole equipment catalogue, every skill list and all 22 warband files — roughly 400 kB of JSON — and the Warbands tab imported it directly for its embedded Rules toggle, so the entire rules corpus shipped to someone who only ever opens their roster. Both embedded rules panels now load on demand. Still above Vite's 500 kB warning: what remains is largely the warband definitions, dragged in by a name lookup, which needs a generated id→name map rather than another lazy boundary.
- ✅ **Who's using it, and pagination where lists grow** — the admin screen gained a player list (counts per person, last activity) and a per-player detail screen showing their warbands and campaigns, so "is anyone actually playing this" has an answer. Email addresses, roster contents and BTB objectives are excluded from every admin function on purpose — owner-only objectives are the reason that table is separate at all, and an admin view that read them would undo it. Lists that grow without limit — the public gallery, the issue inbox, the player list — moved to `useInfiniteQuery` behind a Load more control, replacing a flat `.limit(200)`. The campaign battle log deliberately stayed unpaginated: the standings table derives every player's W/L/D from that same array, so paging it would quietly report wrong records rather than merely showing fewer rows.
- ✅ **Game nights** — the `campaign_events` table had been sitting migrated and unused since the second migration. It now has three screens, because scheduling turned out to answer two different questions: a **list** (where a leader adds and edits), a **month calendar** for "are we free that weekend", and a **detail screen** for one event. Calendar cells carry a dot and a count rather than titles — a 50px square on a phone can't hold "Game night — Session 5", and a truncated title is worse than a mark saying "something is here, tap to read it". No date library: one month grid is about forty lines of arithmetic, and a dependency for it would be larger than the feature. Day keys are built from the *local* date parts rather than `toISOString().slice(0,10)`, which converts to UTC first and files a 9pm game night under the following day for anyone east of Greenwich.
- ✅ **Destructive actions that can't be taken on your behalf** — three related holes. A campaign leader could delete a campaign at any moment, taking every other player's log, standings and game nights; deletion is now blocked while anyone else is still in it, and the screen *explains what to do* rather than hiding the button, because "why can't I delete this" is the question a hidden control leaves unanswered. Counting the members needs a `SECURITY DEFINER` helper, since `campaign_members` carries its own RLS and an inline subquery would be filtered by the caller's own visibility. Deleting a warband that's entered in a campaign now needs a **ticked acknowledgement** as well as the typed name — typing the name proves you know *which* warband you're destroying, not that you've read that it drops out of a campaign's standings — and the checkbox appears only when there really is a second party, since ticking things for their own sake is how people learn to tick without reading. Finally, a mistyped battle result was permanent: the RLS policy had allowed reporter-or-leader deletes since migration 0005 with nothing calling it, so single log entries are now removable behind the row's disclosure. Removing the record doesn't touch the warbands — XP and injuries were applied at commit time.
- ✅ **Printable roster sheet** — a **Print roster sheet** button on each warband opens `/warbands/:id/print`, laid out after the official Games Workshop sheet (1999): the same sections in the same order and its field names, so anyone who has filled one in by hand knows where to look. Ours is drawn from scratch in the app's own type and rules and carries no GW artwork or branding. The Experience track is the real one — 90 boxes in three rows of 30 for a Hero, 14 for a Henchman, thick-bordered at each advance threshold — and since the app knows the totals, the boxes arrive ticked, which is the one thing this has over a photocopy. Dead, captured and departed models are left off; `isInWarband` is now the single definition of who is still in the warband, shared with the rating so the sheet and the number can't disagree. Output goes through the browser's own print path rather than a PDF library: generating a file would mean shipping a layout engine plus embedded Alegreya and IM Fell for one screen, or rasterising with html2canvas, which turns a page made almost entirely of small numbers into a blurry image. Printing keeps the text vector, offers "Save as PDF" everywhere, and also prints. The sheet is black on white on screen as well as on paper — neither theme is printable as it stands, Grimdark being a solid black page and an empty cartridge — and the preview is held to A4-minus-margins so it breaks its lines exactly where the printout will.
- ✅ **The 30-day purge actually runs** — warband deletion has been a soft delete since migration 0009, on the promise that a scheduled job would remove the rows after 30 days. There was no job, so nothing had ever been purged: every warband anyone had deleted was still there, and since photos landed, so was every picture, costing Storage quota indefinitely. It now runs nightly on pg_cron, with a manual trigger on the admin screen. The awkward part isn't deleting rows — it's that `warband_photos` cascades away *with* the warband, so the moment the row goes, the only record of which files belonged to it goes too, leaving bytes in the bucket that nobody can even name. So the paths are copied into a queue before the rows are deleted, in the same transaction. That's the upload rule read backwards: on the way in the row is written last so it never points at missing bytes, and on the way out the row is deleted last so the bytes never outlive the last reference to them. Draining the queue is separate because SQL genuinely can't do it — `storage.objects` is metadata, and deleting a row there doesn't free the file; only the Storage API does, which needs a session. That happens from the admin screen, where a failed drain simply leaves the queue intact to try again.
- ✅ **Warband photos** — a group shot on each warband, shown on its roster and as a thumbnail in the warband list. A warband without a photo shows nothing at all rather than a placeholder — most warbands won't have one, and a silhouette in every row would make the app's ordinary state look like a page of failed image loads. Photos are resized on the device before upload: a 5.5 MB phone photo becomes a ~13 kB WebP at 1600px plus a 480×320 thumbnail, which is the part that matters — Supabase's free tier gives 1 GB of storage but only 2 GB of egress a month, and a list loading full-size images is how that gets spent. No image dependency: `createImageBitmap` decodes and applies EXIF orientation in one step (without which portrait photos arrive on their side), and a canvas re-encodes. The record lives in its own `warband_photos` table rather than inside the warband blob, which the original plan called for: a photo in the blob would make uploading a picture a roster *save*, colliding with pending stat edits and being refused as "changed elsewhere". Storage and Postgres can't share a transaction, so the ordering is the design — the row is only ever written once the bytes it points at exist, and replacing writes a *new* path before deleting the old, since overwriting in place would leave cached URLs serving the previous picture and make a replace look like it had silently failed. **Photos need an account to view**, deliberately narrower than the rest of the gallery: that stays readable signed-out, but user-uploaded images on the open web is a moderation and takedown problem worth avoiding for the price of one sentence. iPhone HEIC files are rejected with the exact setting to change rather than a decoder being shipped for them — silently failing on iPhone uploads is the classic version of this bug.
- ✅ **Co-leaders** — a campaign can have as many leaders as it likes. One leader who stops turning up used to be a dead end: nobody else could rename the campaign, regenerate the join code, schedule a game night or delete it, and there was no way to take over. The only tool was a transfer that handed the role across and demoted you in the same breath. Nothing in the schema had ever required a single leader — the permission check was always "is this user *a* leader", and the leave guard already asked "does another leader remain" — so what was missing was only a way to make a second one and a way back down. Three actions now, each honest about what happens to you: **Make co-leader** (you both lead), **Hand over** (they lead, you don't), and **Step down as leader**, which is deliberately separate from leaving the campaign — conflating those was why handing over used to cost you your seat as well. The rule that a campaign with members always has at least one leader is a database trigger rather than a check inside each function, because those functions aren't the only way that column can ever be written; `campaign_members` has no UPDATE policy at all, so a role can only change through a function that checks the caller first. Any leader can demote any leader, including themselves — an unremovable owner would put back the single point of failure the whole thing exists to remove.
- ✅ **Roster rows show a statline** — rows carried a name and an XP figure but no characteristics, so checking a warrior's Toughness while deploying meant opening him. They now use the same profile block as everywhere else (§5.3), which also exposed that the block's "collapsed" form hard-coded the phone compromise at every width: 198px of table with 4px cells stranded in a 900px card, against the hero detail screen's 860px on the same laptop. Past the phone breakpoint it takes back the printed spacing and fills its container, with the width left to context so it still shrink-wraps where it sits beside a name.

## Tech stack

- **Vite + React + TypeScript**, Tailwind CSS for styling (dark theme by default)
- **Supabase** — Postgres, email/password auth, and row-level security. The sole source of truth for user data; there is no local database and no offline write queue.
- **TanStack Query** for all server data (fetching, caching, invalidation). **Zustand** holds only transient UI state — currently just the in-progress battle session, which is deliberately memory-only so an uncommitted session dies with the tab.
- **react-router-dom** for the screen flow (bottom tab bar on mobile, left sidebar on tablet/desktop)
- **vite-plugin-pwa** for the install manifest and "Add to Home Screen" support

## Getting started

Requires Node 18+ (the `workbox-build` dependency is pinned to a version compatible with Node 18; see the `overrides` field in `package.json`), plus a Supabase project.

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env.local   # then fill in your Supabase URL + anon key
```

Apply the database schema to your Supabase project — this creates every table, their RLS policies, the `SECURITY DEFINER` functions, and the trigger that provisions a `profiles` row on signup:

```bash
npx supabase login
npx supabase link --project-ref <your-project-ref>
npx supabase db push
```

Then the usual:

```bash
npm run dev       # start the dev server
npm run build     # type-check + production build to dist/
npm run preview   # serve the production build locally
```

Sign up through the app's own register screen. Note that Supabase projects have **email confirmation on by default**, so a new account can't sign in until the emailed link is clicked — turn it off under Authentication → Providers → Email if you'd rather not bother for a personal instance.

### Demo mode

Judging a list, a standings table or the public gallery needs more than one player's data in it. `npm run dev` with `?demo=1` on any URL (or the toggle on Account, which is shown to admins only) fills the app with 50 players, 100 warbands and 10 campaigns of 5–10 members each, and signs you in as one of them:

```
http://localhost:5173/campaigns?demo=1     # on
http://localhost:5173/campaigns?demo=0     # off
```

Nothing is written to Supabase. Every call in `src/api` short-circuits to `src/dev/demoApi`, which serves a generated in-memory database — so a hundred warbands never end up in the same tables as your real campaign, in other players' standings, or in the public gallery. Writes made while clicking around land in that in-memory copy and are gone on reload.

The data is seeded, so the same warbands, ratings and standings come back every run, and the rosters are built through the real warband factory — the ratings on the standings table are computed, not invented. It's UI volume, not a test of the queries or of RLS, both of which are replaced.

The whole thing is gated on `import.meta.env.DEV`, which Vite replaces with `false` when building for production, so the generator and its guards are dropped from `dist/` rather than merely being unreachable.

## Project structure

```
src/
  data/            Static game-content data (warbands, equipment, skills, spells/prayers,
                    injuries, advances, racial maximums, shared special rules, wyrdstone
                    prices, Hired Swords, BTB objectives, ...)

  hooks/           TanStack Query hooks the screens actually call (useWarbands,
                    useCampaign, useObjective) — queries, mutations, cache invalidation
  auth/            AuthProvider (session context) and RequireAuth (route gate)
  dev/             Demo mode — a seeded in-memory stand-in for the whole data layer,
                    compiled out of production builds (see "Demo mode" above)
  storage/         Export/import helpers and the jsonb schemaVersion migration path
  store/           Zustand — transient UI state only (the in-progress battle session)
  lib/             Pure helper logic (warband rating, stat lines, wyrdstone pricing, equipment lookup,
                    dice rolling, advance table parsing, skill prerequisite checking, ...)
  components/      Shared UI, including EquipmentShop (the Common/Rare buy flow used by
```

## Data & privacy

Your data lives in Supabase Postgres, tied to your account. Row-level security is enforced at the database level, not just in the UI: warbands, battles, and objectives are readable only by their owner (or by members of a campaign you've joined), and an unauthenticated request can't read or write anything. Entering a warband into a campaign widens who can read it — every member of that campaign, whatever the warband's own public/private setting — and the read-only shared roster relies entirely on that policy to decide what it may show, with no client-side check standing in for it. The public rules pages don't change that — they're served from static JSON bundled into the app and never touch the database, so browsing them signed out queries nothing. BTB objectives sit in their own owner-only table precisely because warbands can be shared with campaign-mates while objectives are meant to stay secret.

The **Account** tab still exports a full JSON backup of everything you own, and can import one back (overwriting your current data, with a confirmation step).

## Game data & verification

Static game-content files under `src/data/` are sourced from the official Mordheim rulebook and the Border Town Burning supplement (via broheim.net's hosted rulebook/BTB PDFs), with the source and page/URL cited in each file's `source` field. Where a value couldn't be confidently verified, it's marked `"TODO: verify vs rulebook p.XX"` rather than guessed — a wrong Strength value is worse than a blank one. Notably:

- `xpThresholds.json` now holds the verified advance thresholds — Heroes at 2, 4, 6, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 41, 46, 51, 57, 63, 69, 76, 83, 90 and Henchmen at 2, 5, 9, 14 — read directly off the official roster sheet's Experience track (the sheet was rasterised and the thick-bordered advance boxes counted by border ink density). The hero/henchmen detail screens now show how much XP remains until the next advance.
- A handful of individual items/skills/Hired Swords are flagged incomplete in their respective files.
- The 5 core skill lists (`skills.json`) and their prerequisites were cross-checked directly against the rulebook's Campaigns chapter. A data bug from an earlier pass was also fixed: Sisters of Sigmar's and Skaven's unique skill lists were referenced by the wrong id from their warband files, so those lists were silently missing from the app — this is now corrected.

If you own the books, please cross-check anything you rely on for a real campaign, and treat the `TODO` markers as a to-do list.

## Deployment

Live at **[mordheimmanager.net](https://mordheimmanager.net)**, deployed as a **Cloudflare Worker serving static assets** — not a Pages project. The two sit together under "Workers & Pages" in the dashboard but take different configuration, and the Worker is the one that needs a file in the repo: [`wrangler.toml`](./wrangler.toml).

| Setting | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Build command | `npm run build` |
| Deploy command | `npx wrangler deploy` |
| Build variables | `VITE_SUPABASE_URL`, `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` |

Those two must be set as **build** variables, not as the Worker's runtime Variables and Secrets. Vite substitutes `import.meta.env` at build time, so a value that only exists at runtime never reaches the bundle — the app builds cleanly and then fails to reach Supabase at all. This happened on the first Cloudflare deploy, and the app now says so on the page rather than rendering a black rectangle with the explanation in the console: missing configuration is reported by `StartupError`, which names the variables and where they go. Both are safe to expose publicly: the anon key grants nothing on its own, since row-level security is what actually gates access.

Routing and caching are split between two files, deliberately:

- **`wrangler.toml`** sets `not_found_handling = "single-page-application"`. Every path in this app is a client-side route, so a request with no file behind it — a bookmark, a shared roster link, a refresh on `/rules/blackpowder` — must serve `index.html` with a 200, or the router never gets to read the URL.
- **`public/_headers`** sets `no-cache` on `/sw.js`, because a cached service worker pins a cached app, which is the shape of the bug that made two correct deploys look broken; and `immutable` on `/assets/*`, whose filenames are content-hashed and can never change meaning. Workers reads this from the assets directory, Netlify from the publish directory, and Vite copies `public/` into `dist/` verbatim, so one file serves both.

A `_redirects` file with `/* /index.html 200` would also give SPA routing, and was the first attempt. It came out again: redirects are evaluated *before* headers and win on a match, so a rule matching `/*` matches `/assets/*` too and would drop the immutable header those files exist to get. `not_found_handling` only engages when nothing was found, so the two never interact.

The old Netlify deployment at `mordheim.builderbasement.com` still builds from the same branch — `netlify.toml` is kept, and the two files above work there too — so the move is reversible until the DNS is retired.

**A domain move needs one thing changed outside this repo:** Supabase → Authentication → URL Configuration. The Site URL and the redirect allow-list gate where auth emails may send people, so until the new domain is listed there, password resets and email confirmations land on the old host or are refused outright. Everything else follows the domain on its own — share links and the password-reset callback are both built from `window.location.origin`.

## Known gaps

- **Offline.** There is none for *data*, by design — everything lives server-side and the app requires a connection. Assets are a separate question and are cached: hashed `/assets/` on CacheFirst, the self-hosted fonts under `/fonts/` on CacheFirst too (they're subset woff2 pulled from Google once by `scripts/fetch-fonts.mjs` and served from our own origin, so the Rulebook look survives offline with no third-party request), same-origin images on StaleWhileRevalidate, the shell on NetworkFirst, Supabase never. What was removed is the precache *manifest*, which is what pinned a stale `index.html` through two deploys — not caching as such. Spec §2 and §12.4.
- **Shared campaigns are untested with a *second* account.** The longest-standing open item. The single-player half is verified end to end against a live account — code issued, warband linked, standings populated with player/rating/record, read-only roster rendered, unlink drops it back out. What no one has exercised yet is two accounts against each other: joining by code, the joiner seeing the shared log and another player's roster, and — the claim the separate owner-only objectives table exists to make — the joiner *not* seeing the owner's BTB objective. Also unconfirmed: that removing a player really drops their warband out of the standings via the 0003 trigger, and that a non-admin second account gets nothing from `/admin`.
- **Account.** Spec §4.6 also calls for a data-file version display and a strict-validation toggle. Not built. Each data file already carries a `schemaVersion` and a `source`, so surfacing them is presentation work rather than plumbing. The "report a data error" link it also asked for is superseded by the report button, which files to a table with the page and build attached instead of opening a mail client.
- **An absent sole leader is still a soft spot.** Co-leaders (0012) are the remedy — but somebody has to appoint one *before* the leader goes quiet. If they vanish while leading alone, there's still no petition or timeout by which the group can take over. Whether that needs solving is a real question: it costs a voting or timeout mechanism, and "ask them to add a co-leader" covers the case as long as the group thinks of it first.
- **Photos are warband-only so far**; heroes, henchmen groups and hired swords have none, and there's no crop UI. Everything was also verified in a desktop browser with synthesised images — the camera path and real iPhone HEIC behaviour haven't been tried on a phone.
- **No quota warning.** The free tier gives 1 GB of storage and 2 GB of egress a month, and nothing reports either. `admin_stats()` counts rows, not bytes, so the first sign of trouble would be images failing to load.
- **Data-file notes carry maintainer TODOs.** A unit's `notes` legitimately holds rules text that belongs on a model ("Prayers of Sigmar (see Magic)"), and the factory copies it onto every recruit. Some data files also use that field for sourcing caveats — "statMaximums null: Beastmen are not Human…" — which therefore reach the model detail screen and the printed roster sheet. Wants a separate field in the data-file schema; deliberately not papered over by filtering strings.
- **The entry bundle is still 666 kB** (180 kB gzipped), over Vite's 500 kB warning. Mostly the 22 warband data files, pulled in by `getWarbandTypeName` — a name lookup dragging 227 kB of definitions. Wants a generated id→name map with a drift check.
- **`fetchWarbands` selects the full jsonb blob** for the owner's list view. Not a one-line narrowing: the roster, add-hero and model screens read from that same query, and `gold`/`wyrdstoneShards` live inside the blob, so it needs two queries with separate cache keys — a refactor touching the save and undo paths.
- **Static-data versioning.** Every data file has a `schemaVersion`, but nothing compares them across releases, so a corrected weapon price can't announce itself in the changelog. Doing it properly means a build-time diff of the data files, not a hand-maintained number.
- **Exploration results the app can't apply.** The wizard rolls the chart and banks the gold and shards, but only those: an Exploration result that hands you a Zombie, a wardog, a free Hired Sword, a training manual or a blessed weapon is reported as text in the battle notes, not applied to the roster. Effects that persist (the Catacombs re-roll, the Straggler's extra die, a Graveyard that makes Witch Hunters hate you) go into the warband's notes and are **not** fed back into the next Exploration roll — nothing reads those notes. Doing it properly means real fields on the warband and a migration, which is why it's deliberately deferred rather than half-modelled.
- **Jewelsmith and Merchant's House gold is left to the player.** Both are worth money only if you sell what you found — the Jewelsmith's gems can instead be kept for +1 on rare item rolls, and the Merchant's House pays nothing at all if the 2D6 comes up a double (you find the Freetraders symbol instead). Auto-applying either would assume a choice the player hasn't made, so they carry no `autoYield`.
- **Per-scenario page references.** `scenarios.json` cites the Scenarios chapter as a whole (p.85–92), not a page per scenario, so the pre-battle screen points at the range. Exact pages need to come from the book.
- **Unit special rules aren't machine-readable yet.** They are structured now — named entries, twelve of them shared across warbands — but nothing *acts* on them: the skill picker and the advance tables still can't consult a rule, because the effect is prose. 39 units also still carry their entry as unsplit source text under a "to do" badge rather than as named rules; that part is transcription work.
- **Purchase prices are complete.** Every purchasable item now resolves to a price. The two that resolve to no *fixed* price — Gromril and Ithilmar weapons — do so correctly, since they cost a multiple of the base weapon rather than a set amount.
