# Mordheim Campaign Manager

A mobile-first Progressive Web App for managing Mordheim warbands and campaigns, including Border Town Burning (BTB) supplement content. Built for use at the game store — install it via "Add to Home Screen" and it behaves like a native app. Your warbands live in a Supabase Postgres database behind an account, so the same campaign follows you across phone, tablet, and desktop.

Full product spec: [`mordheim-manager-spec.md`](./mordheim-manager-spec.md).

## Status

This is under active development. Current state, milestone by milestone:

- ✅ **Scaffold** — Vite + React + TypeScript + Tailwind PWA, offline-capable, deployable to Netlify.
- ✅ **Game data** — warband definitions, equipment, skills, injuries, advance tables, wyrdstone prices, Hired Swords, Dramatis Personae, and BTB campaign objectives, sourced from the core rulebook and Border Town Burning supplement (see [Game data & verification](#game-data--verification) below).
- ✅ **Warbands** — create a warband from any of 14 warband types (the 9 core-rulebook/BTB ones, plus Averlanders, Beastmen Raiders, Carnival of Chaos, Dwarf Treasure Hunters, and Kislevites from The New Mordheimer's Grade 1a lists), manage its roster (Heroes, Henchmen Groups, Hired Swords), edit stats/XP/skills/injuries/equipment, move gear to and from the treasury.
- ✅ **Post-Battle Wizard** — the 8-step guided sequence (battle info → injuries → experience → advances → dead models → income → upkeep → confirm) with a staged diff summary and single-level undo. Dice-driven steps have real roll buttons instead of requiring physical dice: D66 rolls for the Serious Injury table (with the result looked up automatically), D6 rolls for Henchmen casualties and Hired Sword fate, and full 2D6 rolls against the sourced Advance tables (auto-resolving fixed/roll-again results, presenting a choice for "either X or Y" results, and opening the skill picker for "New Skill" results).
- ✅ **Trading Post** — buy Common and Rare equipment (including warband-exclusive items) against the warband's gold, sell items back from the treasury. Rare purchases can be auto-rolled (2D6 against the item's Rare number) or resolved by hand for warband-specific modifiers. Equipment can also be bought directly from a Hero/Hired Sword or Henchmen Group's detail screen, equipping it on that model/group in one step instead of routing through the treasury.
- ✅ **Campaign Log** — start/rename a campaign and toggle Border Town Burning; a chronological, expandable log of every battle committed via the Post-Battle Wizard; a per-warband BTB objective tracker (pick an objective, free-text Campaign Points/progress notes, completed flag) shown when the campaign uses BTB.
- ✅ **Home** — a dashboard tab (first in the bottom nav, the app's `/` route) summarizing the active campaign, all warbands at a glance, and quick links into Post-Battle/Trading/Campaign. The bottom nav is icon-only on narrow phones and brings labels back at wider viewports, so the tabs don't crowd each other.
- ✅ **Pre-Battle & During-Battle** — the Battle tab now starts with setup (pick or randomly roll a scenario, note the opponent by name or by picking another warband stored in the app, pre-battle notes) before the fight, then a live tracker during it (turn counter, a timestamped event log, and a read-only quick-reference roster — stats, equipment, skills — for your own warband or the selected opponent's). Both screens persist through a reload so an accidental refresh at the table doesn't lose the game state. Finishing flows straight into the Post-Battle Wizard, which prefills the scenario/opponent from what was set up.
- ✅ **Skills system** — the 5 core skill lists (Combat, Shooting, Academic, Strength, Speed) plus each warband's unique list are wired into a shared skill picker used by both the model detail screens and the Post-Battle Wizard's Advances step. It hard-blocks skills a Hero already knows or definitely isn't eligible for (leader-only, excluded warband types) and surfaces prerequisites it can't verify (e.g. "Spellcasters only") as a warning instead of guessing.
- ✅ **Rules Reference** — a searchable, offline rules browser covering the full core rulebook: Characteristics, the Turn sequence, Movement, Shooting, Close Combat, Wounds & Injuries, Leadership & Psychology, Weapons & Armour, Miscellaneous Equipment, Magic/casting (plus the wizard-to-lore allocation table), the Post-Battle Sequence, Serious Injuries, Experience (including the Underdog XP bonus table), Trading & Hired Swords, and — generated live from the app's own data rather than duplicated — Skills, Scenarios, Warband-Specific Rules, and Border Town Burning objectives/Dramatis Personae. Every entry is grouped and ordered to match the real rulebook's chapter structure and cites the exact page it came from. Entries cross-link to related rules, and confirmed gaps (per-lore spell lists, the core rulebook's own Dramatis Personae, the more elaborate Optional Rules variants, full scenario terrain/deployment text) are flagged explicitly instead of guessed.
- ✅ **Rules woven into their home tabs** — Warbands, Trading, and Campaign each have a "Rules" toggle alongside their normal view, surfacing just the rules relevant to that tab (warband creation & each faction's special rules; trading & Hired Swords; the post-battle/campaign rules, Serious Injuries, Experience, and Border Town Burning) without leaving the screen. The full Rules Reference (with search) is still always available from its own tab too.
- ✅ **PWA polish** — the iOS-specific "Add to Home Screen" meta tags (standalone status bar, home screen title) alongside the existing Android/manifest support, plus a proper `<meta name="description">`. There is no longer a service worker: installability comes from the manifest and meta tags alone (spec §2). Precaching an app shell made sense when warbands lived in `localStorage`, but since the move to Supabase every screen showing your data needs the network anyway — so the cache bought nothing and cost real confusion, serving an old bundle out of `workbox-precache-v2` after a deploy had landed and making a correct release look broken until the user cleared site data. The build now emits a self-destroying worker that browsers pick up on their next update check; because its cache cleanup runs outside `event.waitUntil` and can be cut short, `clearLegacyServiceWorker` also unregisters and deletes leftover `workbox-*` caches from the page on startup, where nothing can terminate it early.
- ✅ **Responsive tablet/desktop layout** — below `md` (mobile) the app keeps its bottom tab bar and full-width screens. At `md` and up a left sidebar takes over: an icon-only rail on tablet that expands to icons + labels on desktop, with the app wordmark at the top. The screen content sits in a centered, readable max-width column beside it instead of stretching edge-to-edge. Both navs are generated from one shared list of tabs/icons so they never drift apart.
- ✅ **Supabase backend, accounts & multi-device sync** — the app moved off browser `localStorage` onto Supabase Postgres as the single source of truth, so a campaign is no longer trapped on one device. Email/password accounts gate everything that touches *your* data; every screen reads and writes through TanStack Query hooks (`src/hooks`) over a thin API layer (`src/api`). Seven tables (`profiles`, `campaigns`, `campaign_members`, `warbands`, `battles`, `objectives`, `campaign_events`) all have row-level security, so you can only ever see your own data or a campaign you've been invited to. Warband saves use optimistic concurrency on `updated_at` — if the same warband was changed elsewhere, the save is rejected rather than silently clobbering the other edit. The post-battle undo snapshot moved server-side too, becoming per-warband instead of one global slot. BTB objectives live in their own owner-only table rather than inside the warband blob, since warbands are shareable and objectives are secret by the rules.
- ✅ **Public rules, private data** — the reference half of the app needs no account: the home page, the full Rules Reference and every rule entry (skills included), and the changelog are all open, so the rules are usable at the table by anyone. An account is only required for the parts that read or write your own rows — warbands (list, create, roster, model/henchmen detail), the pre/during/post-battle flow, the Trading Post, the campaign log, and data export/import. Signed out, the home page becomes a short landing view pointing at the rules and the sign-in/register links, and Settings hides export/import behind a prompt rather than offering buttons that can't work.
- ✅ **Public warband gallery** — the `public` visibility flag finally has a consumer. The Warbands tab gains a **Public** view listing every shared warband with its owner, type and rating, searchable by warband name, player name, or the *display* name of the type (searching "possessed" finds a `cult-of-the-possessed` warband, which matching the stored slug alone would miss), plus a type filter that only offers types actually present in the results. Rows link to the same read-only roster the campaign standings use, now at the neutral `/rosters/:id` since a shared warband isn't necessarily a campaign one. Fixed alongside it: `warbandType` stores the definition slug, but six screens rendered it raw, so the app showed "cult-of-the-possessed" wherever it meant "Cult of the Possessed". A single `getWarbandTypeName` helper resolves it, falling back to the raw value so an unrecognised or legacy type still shows something.
- ✅ **Two themes, and the app's own icon** — Settings → Appearance offers **Grimdark** (the default: near-black stone, bone text, ember accent) and **Rulebook** (aged paper, black serif ink, dark sepia accent — the printed book, and far easier to read under bright light). The palette moved behind CSS variables so a theme is a set of variable values rather than a rewrite of the ~1200 utility classes that reference it; the surface *roles* are unchanged, so `ink-950` is still "the page" and `ink-900` still "a card" — they're simply both light in Rulebook, with `ink-800/700` becoming darker rules on paper instead of lighter edges in the dark. Applied by an inline script before first paint so a Rulebook user never sees a flash of the dark theme, and stored per device: reading at a bright table in a game store is a different problem from reading at home, and it shouldn't follow the account to another screen. Contrast was measured rather than eyeballed — every text/background pair clears WCAG AA in both themes, most at AAA. Also fixes `bone-400`, used in 29 places but never defined, so those hints silently inherited their parent's colour instead of going dimmer. The home-screen icon is generated from a single source into the sizes Android and iOS need, with the maskable variant re-cropped to sit inside the 80% safe zone (the artwork had 6% top margin and would have lost the sword hilt to a circular mask).
- ✅ **Explicit saving** — typed fields used to write on every keystroke, so correcting a characteristic fired one save per digit and there was no moment at which the user decided the edit was finished; a mis-tap had no undo, and a dropped connection surfaced as an alert mid-typing. The roster, hero/Hired Sword, henchmen and campaign screens now hold a local draft with a save bar that appears only when something is pending. Discrete actions — buying, recruiting, selling, deleting, recording an advance or injury — deliberately still write immediately, since they *are* the user's decision and putting a second Save behind a Buy button reads as the purchase not having happened. Those actions apply on top of the current draft rather than beside it, so a screen never holds two competing versions of the warband and pending field edits are carried along instead of being lost. Leaving the tab with unsaved changes prompts.
- ✅ **Caching that can't strand a deploy** — query results are held for 30s with a 5-minute retention, so moving around your own roster stops refetching on every tap (window focus and mutations still invalidate, so a campaign-mate's change lands promptly). The service worker is back, but built the opposite way round from the one that broke two releases: **no precache manifest at all**, the HTML shell on NetworkFirst so a new deploy wins the moment there's a connection, hashed `/assets/` on CacheFirst because their contents can never change, and Supabase calls explicitly uncached — your warbands must never come from a stale copy. Navigation is cached under a single key, since Netlify rewrites every path to the same HTML; without that, opening `/rules` offline would miss unless that exact URL had been visited online. Verified by stopping the server and loading a deep link that had never been opened: the full Rules Reference rendered with all 20 chapters.
- ✅ **Battle flow** — the pre-battle opponent picker offers every warband entered in your campaign alongside your own, grouped and labelled with the owning player since two people can field the same warband type; picking one resolves through the shared read-only path, because another player's warband isn't in the `owner_id = me` query the screen was using. Casualties are recorded as they happen: Heroes and Hired Swords toggle out of action, henchmen groups get a counter (a group is N models behind one card, so *how many* went down is the only answerable question), and the tally seeds the post-battle Injuries step instead of being reconstructed from memory afterwards. Only out-of-action carries over — who *died* is a post-battle roll — and it's re-derived against the current warband, so ids that no longer exist are dropped and group counts clamped. Stepping the turn counter back is confirmed, since it's the one number nobody can reconstruct and the button sits a thumb's width from "+".
- ✅ **Laid out like the book** — equipment is grouped under the price chart's own headings in the price chart's own order (hand-to-hand, missile, armour, miscellaneous) in the shop, the treasury and everywhere else gear is listed, so looking something up matches the muscle memory of the printed page; an unrecognised category falls into Miscellaneous rather than vanishing, so a data typo surfaces as a misfiled item. Henchmen are listed out individually beneath their group — the rules treat a group as one entity and the data holds a count, but "4x Swordsman" doesn't read like four bodies on the table — while everything editable stays on the group, and their Experience is labelled "each" because it isn't a group total. The Rules Reference browses as a collapsible chapter list with per-chapter counts; search deliberately stays flat, since collapsing would hide the matches. The disclosure arrow is a real SVG instead of a `▶` text glyph that reflowed differently in every substituted system font.
- ✅ **Exploration tables** — the full Exploration chart (all 30 double-through-six-of-a-kind results, with their sub-tables and item checklists), the wyrdstone shards table and the magical artefacts table, transcribed from the owner's own rulebook and browsable in the Rules Reference.
- ✅ **Exploration in the post-battle wizard** — the Income step now rolls the chart instead of asking for a wyrdstone total from memory. The dice pool is derived from the draft (one per Hero who survived without going out of action, plus one for a win) and can be adjusted for skills or equipment that grant more; each die is a picker as well as a roll button, since the rules expect players to witness each other's dice and an Elf Ranger's +1/-1 or a Mordheim Map re-roll has to land somewhere. Past six dice the extras become tappable Counts/Dropped chips, because the rulebook keeps only six however many you rolled. Multiples are matched with the book's own tie-breaks (most numerous wins, then highest face), and the result is resolved *for your warband* — Skaven sell the Straggler for 2D6 gc where everyone else interrogates him, Witch Hunters seal the Graveyard for Experience where everyone else loots it for D6x10. What the app applies on its own is deliberately limited to gold and shards, declared as an `autoYield` dice expression in the data rather than parsed out of the prose; everything else (a Zombie, a wardog, a free Hired Sword, a blessed weapon) is written into the battle notes for the player, and effects that outlast the battle are appended to the warband's notes as well.
- ✅ **Weapon slot limits** — two close combat weapons plus the free dagger, and two *different* missile weapons, per the rulebook's equipment section. Both details in that sentence matter: the free dagger is identified by costing nothing, so a bought one still counts against the limit, and counting distinct missile *names* is what makes "a brace of pistols counts as a single missile weapon" fall out naturally — a second pistol is the same weapon, not a third slot. Armour and miscellaneous gear stay uncapped. Enforced on both routes onto a model (buying and assigning from the treasury), and shown as `1/2 close combat, 0/2 missile` above the equipment list so a full slot is visible before shopping rather than at the point of refusal. The treasury is deliberately exempt: the rules cap what a warrior *carries*, not what the warband owns. Warbands already over the limit keep what they have — the check guards additions only, since silently discarding bought gear isn't the app's call.
- ✅ **Several campaigns per player** — the schema always allowed it (`campaign_members` is keyed on campaign *and* user for exactly this reason) and joining several already worked, but the create form lived in the no-campaign branch of the campaign screen, so a leader was stuck with the first one they made. A new **My Campaigns** screen at `/campaigns` is now the Campaign tab's destination, listing every campaign with your role, its player and battle counts, how many of your warbands are entered, and — for leaders only, since nobody else can hand it out — the join code. Both the join field and the start form sit on that screen rather than linking away to the campaign detail. Two navigation faults surfaced while wiring it, of the same kind: `NavLink` matches on a tab's own path, so the Campaign tab went dark the moment you opened a campaign (`/campaign` isn't a prefix of `/campaigns`), and the Warbands tab claimed the entire battle flow, which lives under `/warbands/:id/…` because it belongs to a warband. Nav items can now both claim and *exclude* paths, with exclusion winning over the built-in prefix match.
- ✅ **Equipment rules, restrictions and unit special rules** — three separate faults, found by auditing rather than guessing. Warband-exclusive gear could *never* display its rules: the name→rule index was built only from the universal catalogue, so the Ogre club, hand-held mortar and harpoon crossbow rendered as plain rows while their full text sat unused in the data. Indexing exclusive gear by id and display name surfaced **42 items** at a stroke; a `TODO:` placeholder deliberately still resolves to nothing, so a data gap can't pose as rules. The catalogue then grew **60 → 142** with the whole Miscellaneous Equipment section, of which 36 items are locked to warbands the app doesn't carry yet — present and searchable, correct the day that warband lands, and filtered out of everyone else's shop by `warbandIds`. Purchasing now follows the book: a Henchmen group buys one item *per model* (it charged once regardless of size, so five swordsmen cost one sword), Miscellaneous gear is Heroes-only bar the exceptions the rules name, and each warrior sees only his own entry's list — widened by Weapons Training for hand-to-hand and Weapons Expert for missiles, verified to open the right category and only that one. The treasury is deliberately exempt, because the rules restrict *use*, not purchase. Finally, unit special rules moved out of the free-text `notes` blob into a structured, expandable list — 111 rules across 61 units. Mechanical splitting needed three corrections to be trustworthy: lower-case data remarks weren't recognised as boundaries and bled into the preceding rule; rules written without a colon ("Large (causes fear...)") were being swallowed whole, so one Maneaters rule claimed four unrelated things; and a bare rule is a single sentence, so anything after it belongs elsewhere. What's left unsplit stays visible under a "to do" badge rather than being dropped or dressed up as finished.
- ✅ **§5.4 audited on the real screens** — the design sandbox can't see the app chrome or the detail screens, and four genuine violations were only visible once signed in and measuring the live DOM. `BackHeader` set its blackletter title at 20px, under the §5.2 floor of 24 that exists because blackletter stops being readable below it. Tab-bar labels sat at 11px, under the 12px floor. Inline actions — "Move to treasury", the weapon-rule expanders, checkboxes, the changelog link — ranged from 16 to 24px tall. Worst was the characteristic field at **15px**: the control you most need to hit one-handed at a table was the smallest thing in the app. All are now at or above 44px, and nothing anywhere renders below 12px. Deliberately left alone: a 36–40px convention on tab buttons, Buy buttons and rules filters. §5.4 asks for 48px+, so these fall short, but raising them lengthens dense screens (the trading post has 26) — a density trade-off worth making on purpose rather than by sweep.
- ✅ **The "Rulebook" design language (spec §5)** — aged parchment, heavy woodcut ink, blackletter display type and blood red, sitting alongside the existing Grimdark theme rather than replacing it. The §5.1 tokens are defined per theme so one component works in both: `parchment` means "the page" and `ink` means "text on it", which under Grimdark resolves to bone-on-black. Contrast was computed rather than eyeballed — ink on parchment 12.80:1, white on blood 10.35:1, both clearing WCAG AA with room to spare. Measuring *both* themes turned up a trap the spec's own wording hides: §5 requires white on blood, but Grimdark's accent is a light ember orange where white manages only 3.76:1, so a shared component cannot hardcode white. A paired `on-accent` token resolves to whatever is legible on the accent in the active theme. The §5.2 type system (Pirata One, IM Fell English, Alegreya, Alegreya Sans) is applied once as a base layer rather than opted into by each of fifty-odd files, and is global to both themes — fonts aren't colours, and two parallel type systems would double the work for nothing. The signature element is the profile block: a real `<table>`, not a grid of divs, so a screen reader announces "WS 4" instead of a bare "4"; tabular *and* lining figures, because Alegreya's default oldstyle numerals drop 3/4/5/9 below the baseline and render 0 at x-height, which makes a statline's zeros read as the letter o; and cell padding, never font size, is what shrinks to fit nine columns onto a 375px phone. It's used everywhere a statline appears, editable on the detail screens, which also retired stat labels set at 9 and 10px — under the §5.4 floor.
- ✅ **Advances follow the Experience track** — the wizard offered an advance to every model that gained any XP at all, which is why it handed out far more than a campaign should; `advancesDue()` now counts the Experience boxes actually crossed between the pre- and post-battle totals, so gaining a point from 2 to 3 correctly yields nothing while 1 to 4 yields two. Racial maximums are enforced rather than annotated — the previous ⚠ was advisory and still applied the increase — and staged increases count toward the cap, so two advances in one battle can't both be spent on a characteristic with one point of headroom. A rolled characteristic that's already maxed reports that a re-roll is due instead of being silently wasted, matching the rule quoted in `advances.json`. Every staged advance is removable, since rolling was otherwise irreversible and a mis-tap meant restarting the wizard. Dead heroes, wiped-out henchmen groups and Hired Swords killed in battle no longer collect XP or advances. "That Lad's Got Talent" is recordable at last — its handler was an empty function, so the result could not even be clicked — and resolves on commit by moving one member out of the group into the Heroes with his characteristics and Experience intact; which skill lists he may use varies by warband and isn't in the data, so that's left to the player rather than guessed.
- ✅ **Recruiting obeys the rules** — heroes, henchmen and Hired Swords all cost gold, and the warband's maximum size is enforced alongside each unit's own limit: a henchmen group could previously be sized straight past it, so 30 models fitted into a warband capped at 15 without a word. Full units grey out rather than vanishing, since a silently missing option reads as a bug. Hired Swords are recruitable at last — fee, upkeep, statline, eligibility and special rules shown before you commit — and they correctly sit *outside* the size cap, which is why `countTowardWarbandSize` is deliberately separate from `countModels` in `rating.ts`: rating counts them, roster capacity doesn't. Every warrior starts with the rulebook's free dagger, and each recruit now carries its unit's own rules text (Leader, Large, Ranger, …) which the factory previously discarded, leaving abilities a model has from the moment you hire him nowhere on his card. Warband lists are ordered A–Z and labelled with source and grade, derived from the `source` citation each data file already carries rather than a second hand-maintained field that could drift. Also fixes a rating bug: a henchmen group's Experience is per model — they advance together and share one value — but was counted once for the whole group, understating five 4-XP veterans as 29 instead of 45.
- ✅ **Public warbands need no account** — a roster marked public is now readable signed out, which is the point of publishing one; a link you can't open without registering isn't shared. Enforced in the database rather than the UI: migration 0004 grants `anon` select on warbands whose `visibility = 'public'` and on `profiles` (display names only — the table holds no contact details), leaving the owner and campaign-member branches on the authenticated policy. Verified signed out: public warbands and names readable, private warbands/campaigns/battles/objectives all return zero rows.
- ✅ **No campaign appears by itself** — committing a battle used to silently create a campaign named "My Campaign", because `battles` had nowhere to file a row without one, so every player ended up leading a campaign they never started. Migration 0005 lets a battle exist with no campaign (personal, readable only by its reporter), which also delivers one-off games outside a campaign. The join-code field now sits permanently on the Campaign screen instead of under the Players tab, where nobody found it once they already had a campaign — which read as "you can only be in one".
- ✅ **Reliable edits and typable number fields** — two fixes to things that made ordinary use feel broken. Every warband write carries the `updated_at` it last saw so a second device can't silently clobber your changes, but two saves from the *same* tab were indistinguishable from that: buying an item and buying another before the first response landed meant the second still held the pre-purchase timestamp, so it was refused and the user was told their warband "was changed elsewhere" when nothing of the sort had happened — and the purchase was lost. Saves are now queued per warband and each writes its fresh timestamp straight into the cache, so a run of edits lands in order while the concurrency check still catches a genuinely concurrent editor. Separately, every number field was spelled `value={n} onChange={set(clamp(Number(e.target.value)))}`, which forces each keystroke back through a number and so can never be empty: clearing the field to type a new value snapped it to the minimum under the cursor, and `Number('')` being 0 (with `Math.max(1, NaN)` being NaN) meant a half-typed entry could reach the warband as 0 or NaN. A single `NumberInput` now holds the in-progress text and interprets it only when it parses cleanly and sits in range, rounding and clamping on blur — used by the henchmen group size, the buy and sell price overrides, characteristics, wyrdstone, and the post-battle casualty counts.
- ✅ **Shared campaigns** — a campaign is no longer a private notebook. Creating one issues a short, WhatsApp-shareable join code (`MRDH-7F2K`, drawn from an alphabet with the confusable characters removed — no `0`/`O`, `1`/`I`/`L`, `5`/`S`, `8`/`B` — since these get read aloud and typed on phones); the leader can regenerate it to close off further joins. Codes are matched case- and punctuation-insensitively, so `mrdh7f2k` works. Players enter a code from the Campaign tab (on the create-or-join screen if they have no campaign, or under Players if they already do) and are switched into the campaign they joined. The code can be pushed straight into the group chat: WhatsApp gets a real share link (`wa.me/?text=`), while Discord — which has no share-intent URL, so nothing can open it with a message prefilled — gets a button that copies a Discord-formatted message instead of pretending otherwise. Both carry the app's URL alongside the code, since a bare code is useless to someone who hasn't been told where to enter it. A warband is entered into a campaign from its own roster screen, and then appears in **Standings**. That table is built from the member list outwards rather than from the linked warbands: listing warbands and reading the player off each one is the obvious approach but drops anyone who hasn't entered one — most often the leader, who tends to set the campaign up before building a roster, and so appeared nowhere in their own campaign. Every player is now listed with their name, warband, rating and W/L/D read off the shared battle log, with a Leader badge and an explicit "no warband entered" where that applies, and any member can open a read-only view of anyone's roster (including their own, which is labelled as the view everyone else gets rather than as someone else's warband). Campaign name/BTB/notes are editable by the leader only, matching what RLS already enforced. BTB objectives stay owner-only regardless of who can see the warband. Two database-level guarantees back this up rather than trusting the client: a warband can only be linked to a campaign its owner actually belongs to (a `WITH CHECK` the original schema was missing, without which a client could have pushed its warband into any campaign it knew the id of), and leaving or being removed from a campaign withdraws that player's warbands from it via trigger, since the leader has no write access to someone else's rows.
- ✅ **Sign in/out from the nav, and password recovery** — the navigation carries a single control that reads "Sign in" or "Sign out" depending on session state, pinned to the bottom of the sidebar rail on tablet/desktop and sitting as the last cell of the mobile tab bar. It stays blank while the session is still being restored, so a signed-in user reloading the page never sees a "Sign in" flash. Signing in remembers the page you came from and returns you there. A forgotten-password flow now runs end to end: a link on the sign-in screen leads to a request form, Supabase emails a recovery link back to `/reset-password`, and that screen sets the new password against the temporary recovery session (with expired/reused links reported plainly). The request form deliberately shows the same "if an account exists" message whether or not the address is registered, so it can't be used to discover who has an account. The standalone Skills tab was retired — all 133 skill entries still live in the Rules Reference under their own chapter, which is where people were looking for them anyway.

## Tech stack

- **Vite + React + TypeScript**, Tailwind CSS for styling (dark theme by default)
- **Supabase** — Postgres, email/password auth, and row-level security. The sole source of truth for user data; there is no local database and no offline write queue.
- **TanStack Query** for all server data (fetching, caching, invalidation). **Zustand** holds only transient UI state — currently just the in-progress battle session, which is deliberately memory-only so an uncommitted session dies with the tab.
- **react-router-dom** for the screen flow (bottom tab bar on mobile, left sidebar on tablet/desktop)
- **vite-plugin-pwa** for the install manifest and "Add to Home Screen" support

## Getting started

Requires Node 18+ (the `workbox-build` dependency is pinned to a version compatible with Node 18; see the `overrides` field in `package.json`), plus a Supabase project.

```bash
npm install
cp .env.example .env.local   # then fill in your Supabase URL + anon key
```

Apply the database schema to your Supabase project — this creates all seven tables, their RLS policies, and the trigger that provisions a `profiles` row on signup:

```bash
npx supabase login
npx supabase link --project-ref <your-project-ref>
npx supabase db push
```

Then the usual:

```bash
npm run dev       # start the dev server
npm run build     # type-check + production build to dist/
npm run preview   # serve the production build locally
```

## Data & privacy

Your data lives in Supabase Postgres, tied to your account. Row-level security is enforced at the database level, not just in the UI: warbands, battles, and objectives are readable only by their owner (or by members of a campaign you've joined), and an unauthenticated request can't read or write anything. Entering a warband into a campaign widens who can read it — every member of that campaign, whatever the warband's own public/private setting — and the read-only shared roster relies entirely on that policy to decide what it may show, with no client-side check standing in for it. The public rules pages don't change that — they're served from static JSON bundled into the app and never touch the database, so browsing them signed out queries nothing. BTB objectives sit in their own owner-only table precisely because warbands can be shared with campaign-mates while objectives are meant to stay secret.

The **Settings** tab still exports a full JSON backup of everything you own, and can import one back (overwriting your current data, with a confirmation step).

## Game data & verification

Static game-content files under `src/data/` are sourced from the official Mordheim rulebook and the Border Town Burning supplement (via broheim.net's hosted rulebook/BTB PDFs), with the source and page/URL cited in each file's `source` field. Where a value couldn't be confidently verified, it's marked `"TODO: verify vs rulebook p.XX"` rather than guessed — a wrong Strength value is worse than a blank one. Notably:

- `xpThresholds.json` now holds the verified advance thresholds — Heroes at 2, 4, 6, 8, 11, 14, 17, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36, 41, 46, 51, 57, 63, 69, 76, 83, 90 and Henchmen at 2, 5, 9, 14 — read directly off the official roster sheet's Experience track (the sheet was rasterised and the thick-bordered advance boxes counted by border ink density). The hero/henchmen detail screens now show how much XP remains until the next advance.
- A handful of individual items/skills/Hired Swords are flagged incomplete in their respective files.
- The 5 core skill lists (`skills.json`) and their prerequisites were cross-checked directly against the rulebook's Campaigns chapter. A data bug from an earlier pass was also fixed: Sisters of Sigmar's and Skaven's unique skill lists were referenced by the wrong id from their warband files, so those lists were silently missing from the app — this is now corrected.

If you own the books, please cross-check anything you rely on for a real campaign, and treat the `TODO` markers as a to-do list.

## Known gaps

- **Offline.** There is none, by design — all data is server-side and the app requires a connection. Precaching was removed rather than left to imply otherwise.
- **Campaign events.** The `campaign_events` table and its RLS policies exist and are migrated, but the UI from spec §4.5 — game nights with a date/time picker, and a banner for the next upcoming one — isn't built.
- **Shared campaigns are untested with a *second* account.** The single-player half is verified end to end against a live account — code issued, warband linked, standings populated with player/rating/record, read-only roster rendered, unlink drops it back out. What no one has exercised yet is two accounts against each other: joining by code, the joiner seeing the shared log and another player's roster, and — the claim the separate owner-only objectives table exists to make — the joiner *not* seeing the owner's BTB objective. Also unconfirmed: that removing a player really drops their warband out of the standings via the 0003 trigger.
- **Settings.** Spec §4.6 also calls for a data-file version display, a "report a data error" link, and a strict-validation toggle. Not built. Each data file already carries a `schemaVersion` and a `source`, so surfacing them is presentation work rather than plumbing.
- **Static-data versioning.** Every data file has a `schemaVersion`, but nothing compares them across releases, so a corrected weapon price can't announce itself in the changelog. Doing it properly means a build-time diff of the data files, not a hand-maintained number.
- **Exploration results the app can't apply.** The wizard rolls the chart and banks the gold and shards, but only those: an Exploration result that hands you a Zombie, a wardog, a free Hired Sword, a training manual or a blessed weapon is reported as text in the battle notes, not applied to the roster. Effects that persist (the Catacombs re-roll, the Straggler's extra die, a Graveyard that makes Witch Hunters hate you) go into the warband's notes and are **not** fed back into the next Exploration roll — nothing reads those notes. Doing it properly means real fields on the warband and a migration, which is why it's deliberately deferred rather than half-modelled.
- **Jewelsmith and Merchant's House gold is left to the player.** Both are worth money only if you sell what you found — the Jewelsmith's gems can instead be kept for +1 on rare item rolls, and the Merchant's House pays nothing at all if the 2D6 comes up a double (you find the Freetraders symbol instead). Auto-applying either would assume a choice the player hasn't made, so they carry no `autoYield`.
- **Per-scenario page references.** `scenarios.json` cites the Scenarios chapter as a whole (p.85–92), not a page per scenario, so the pre-battle screen points at the range. Exact pages need to come from the book.
- **Structured unit special rules.** Each unit's rules text now reaches the model, but as the source's prose (which also contains the hire cost). Feeding it to the skill picker or the advance tables needs a real `specialRules` field in the warband data, which is source work.
- **Purchase prices are complete.** Every purchasable item now resolves to a price. The two that resolve to no *fixed* price — Gromril and Ithilmar weapons — do so correctly, since they cost a multiple of the base weapon rather than a set amount.
